Had a long call today with a retailer who wanted us to put "dermatologist tested" on our moisturiser pack because apparently it lifts sales a lot. I asked what the test would actually involve and it turns out it usually just means a dermatologist supervised a patch test on maybe 20-30 people for 48 hours, which tells you it probably won't cause immediate irritation on healthy skin but says nothing about whether the product actually works, or how it behaves on sensitive or acne prone skin, or over months of use. It's not a lie, it's legal, but it sounds like a much bigger claim than it is. We did run a patch test on the moisturiser but we haven't done any efficacy study yet so I said no to the label for now. Retailer wasn't thrilled, and honestly I get the commercial logic, shelf space is brutal. What I'd tell people is when you see "dermatologist tested" ask how many people, for how long, on what skin types, and tested for what exactly - safety or results. If the brand can't answer that's worth knowing.
